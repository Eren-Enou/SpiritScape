# v0.10: Plank Roads

**Player problem.** Training Woodworking by hand makes far more planks than anything uses. In v0.9 the reference game ended with Main Storage full, and beams were about to stop coming home.

**The measurements say roads fix that up to Woodworking 10, but they don't raise steady output.** The reference game now spends 1,315 planks instead of 565 and ends with half of Main Storage free. Nobody has played any of this, so whether roads feel worth buying is unknown.

## Design

Four named routes, planned in the Projects tab (`B`) like any building. Each costs planks only, and everyone crosses a road tile a third faster: 0.2 seconds instead of 0.3.

| Road | Tiles | Development | Normal game | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grove Lane | 12 | 30 planks | 120 | Nothing |
| North Road | 14 | 35 | 140 | Grove Lane |
| Old Growth Road | 28 | 65 | 260 | North Road |
| Forest Road | 24 | 60 | 240 | North Road |

- **Real goods:** the Construction Spirit carries every plank to the works, which stand at the near end of the road. The whole road appears when it's finished.
- **Routes are fixed:** they're lists of corners in `data/defs.toml`. Choosing your own might be better, but needs a way to draw on the map.
- **Pathfinding:** once a road exists, walkers take the quickest route instead of the shortest, so they'll step out of their way to use one. Before that the old path search runs untouched, so older runs repeat exactly.
- **Obstacles:** anything standing on a route stays, and the road runs around it. Nothing can be built on a road.
- **Advice:** when planks are what's filling Main Storage, the storage message names the next road.

### Why roads

I wanted something a player might buy even if storage weren't full. Everyone's walking gets quicker, including the player's, and it reuses the plan, demand and construction already built. A disposal button or a bigger store would only have hidden the problem.

### Rejected

- **Road wear and repairs:** this would be a permanent use of planks, but it adds a chore. It's the first thing to try if roads run out too soon.
- **A cheaper first road:** Grove Lane at 120 planks is meant to be bought with surplus, not saved for.

## Measured

Reproduce with:

```bash
cargo run -p spirit_sim --release --example road_report
```

| Trip for the player | Without | With roads |
|---|---|---|
| Main Storage to the Starting Grove | 3.3s | 2.2s |
| Main Storage to the bridge | 7.8s | 5.2s |
| Main Storage to Old Growth | 13.2s | 9.7s |
| Main Storage to the Forest Crossing | 16.5s | 11.7s |
| Main Storage to the Distant Forest | 15.6s | 11.2s |

30 minutes after a 15-minute warm-up, with every road laid before the start:

| Settlement | Hardwood/min | Beams home/min | Hauler walking | At bench |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original, central | 10.8 | 4.8 | 41% | 42% |
| Original, central, with roads | 10.8 | 4.9 | 34% | 42% |
| Expanded, central | 15.0 | 8.2 | 71% | 68% |
| Expanded, central, with roads | 15.3 | 7.9 | 54% | 66% |
| Expanded, remote | 15.1 | 8.1 | 52% | 66% |
| Expanded, remote, with roads | 15.3 | 8.1 | 51% | 66% |

- **Roads don't raise steady output.** Regrowth is still the limit. Expanded central is 0.3 beams a minute lower with roads, and I haven't looked into why.
- **They free a central hauler,** from 71% walking to 54%. That's the main steady benefit.
- **A remote hauler barely changes,** 52% to 51%. I haven't confirmed why. The likely reason is that it goes more often with smaller loads.
- **Central and remote are now closer** on hauler time, which makes the question of what remote is for more pressing.

The reference player still has hand-written storage habits. Grove Lane is the only road it buys while training, and it lays the others after the Annex.

## What this doesn't solve

- **Roads are a one-off 760 planks.** Woodworking 10 to 20 needs about 124,000 more XP, which is thousands of planks at 25 XP each. Anyone training past level 10 will clog the store again. That needs a recipe that uses planks as an input, and it's the next thing to do.
- **What would count as failure:** Main Storage still full at the end of the route, or the route getting slower. Neither happened. Roads nobody wants to buy would also be a failure, and only a person can show that.

## Saves

- The format is now **version 6**. It only adds defaulted fields, but older games can't read road terrain.
- **Tested on real files:** two fixtures written by the unmodified v0.9 code, one with a plan under way and one with the crossing open. Both load, finish what they were doing and can plan roads. The original is kept as `world.save.v5`.
- **Tested across a save:** a finished road, a road under way, and walkers on a road all continue identically.

## Validation

- `cargo test --workspace`: **353 pass**, 327 from before plus 26 in `tests/roads.rs`. No existing assertion changed.
- Two more tests pass in release only: the normal route, and the new check that it ends with room.
- `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets` is clean.
- **Fourteen UI scripts pass,** including new `ui_roads.txt`.
- I inspected screenshots of the choices, a laid road close up and the overview.
- **Problems found along the way:** the screenshots showed finished roads keeping a floating name over Main Storage's label, and clicking the tile a road started from treated it as a building. A test showed roads paving under buildings. All three are fixed.
- The repo has no CI, so all of this ran locally.

## Not verified

- Physical mouse and keyboard.
- **Whether roads feel worth buying, or whether faster walking is noticeable.**
- Whether the fixed routes go where a person wants to go.
- How the speed change looks when a walker steps on and off a road.

## Technical debt

- A road appears all at once. Laying it tile by tile would look better.
- The Projects tab lists plannable buildings by hand in `ui.rs`, so each new one needs a line there.
- Hauler tiles are estimated from walking time, which undercounts on roads, so `road_report` shows walking share instead.
- Speed rounds to whole ticks, so `road_pct` only has a few useful values.

## Human playtest
