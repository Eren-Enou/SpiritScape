# v0.49: Last time

**Player problem.** In v0.48 you can carve a stone of your own, "for whoever comes next". But nobody comes next. Start a new game and the stone is gone with the old save. The old stones are the story of someone who was here before you. Nothing in the game lets that someone be you.

**Now a stone you carve is kept, and your next new game has it standing beside the first old stone.** Nobody has played it.

## Design

- **When you carve a stone in a real game,** its words are kept in `stones.txt` in the save folder, beside the settings. Scenarios never keep one.
- **A new game stands up to three of them, newest first,** a step or two from the Worn Stone, the first of the old ones. They look like the stone you carved: pale, and a little shorter than the old ones.
- **Walk up to one and you find it:** "You know these chisel marks. You left this stone, last time:" and then its words, exactly as you carved them.
- **Finding one changes what you say next time.** The stone you carve in this game adds "I found the one I left before. It was still there." Both endings' closing words add "You found the stone you left last time. It was still standing."
- **The notes** (`L`) get a section, "What you left last time", once you've found one.

### What I decided

- **The Worn Stone, because it's the first thing the old carver wrote:** "It was the best day." A new player is likely to find it early, and yours stands beside it.
- **Three games at most,** so it stays a few stones and not a graveyard.
- **The file is plain text,** one stone after another with `---` between. A damaged or edited file only ever means fewer stones. Each brings at most six lines of 200 characters, so nothing can fill the screen.
- **Only a real game keeps its stone.** Trying the ending in a scenario shouldn't leave something in your next real game.
- **Nothing else carries over.** No XP, no spirits, nothing that makes the next game easier. It's a stone.

**What would count as failure:** a stone from a scenario showing up in a real game, a stone found twice, or a damaged file stopping the game.

## Not verified

- **Whether a real second game finds the first one's stone.** The pieces are tested: keeping it, reading it back, a new game standing it up, and finding it. But nobody has played two games in a row.
- Whether anyone walks to the Worn Stone in a second game, when they already know what it says.
