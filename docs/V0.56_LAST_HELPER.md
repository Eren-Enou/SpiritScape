# v0.56: The last helper

**Player problem.** The old stones tell of someone who gave everything to their helpers, built no hearth, and was left with nothing that was theirs. The last stone, out past the Hollow Grove, ends it:

> No name on this one. The chisel marks are very neat, the way a helper would make them. We built no hearth. Nobody was left who thought of it.

That's the end of their story, and the strongest line in the game. But nothing on the map answers it. The helpers it describes are nowhere. The player reads it and walks away.

**Now one of them is still there.** Nobody has played it.

## Design

### What you find

- **Once the Last Stone has been read, someone can be seen beside it:** a pale, grey figure carving, with "The last helper" over its head.
- **Walk up to it and it stops and looks up at you:** "Someone is standing by the Last Stone with a chisel in its hand. It's one of theirs: the last of the helpers whoever cut the old stones made. It stops, and looks up at you."
- **You get a question, with two answers, in the What's next card:** "Ask it to come home", or "Leave it where it is". It waits for as long as you like.

### What each answer does

- **Ask it home:** "It puts the chisel down and follows you home. It doesn't work any more. It stands by the fire, and watches." It stands beside your Hearth if it's lit, or else by Main Storage.
- **Leave it:** "You leave it by its stone. When you look back, it's carving again."
- **Both endings remember it.** The Hearth's closing words say "The last of the old helpers stood by your hearth. It had never seen one." or "...is still out by its stone, carving." The Still Boat's say "...crossed with them. Nobody had called it home in a very long time." or "...stayed by its stone. Nobody called it, and it didn't ask."
- **The notes** (`L`) get a section, "The last helper".

### What I decided

- **It does no work, whatever you answer.** A free worker would make "ask it home" the obvious choice. This way the only reason to ask is that you want to.
- **It isn't one of your spirits.** It has no skill, no story card, and it doesn't count anywhere. It's the old carver's, not yours.
- **The answer is for good.**
- **The rules and every line are in `data/defs.toml`, under `[last_helper]`.**

**What would count as failure:** seeing it before the Last Stone is read, being asked twice, it working, or an ending that forgets.

## Measured

The reference game never walks to the Last Stone, so nothing it measures changes: all 19 goals by 16:57:21, and two runs are identical.

## Validation

- `cargo test --workspace`: **685 pass**, 677 from before plus 8 new.
- Eight release-only long-run tests pass.
- `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` is clean.
- **All 45 UI scripts pass,** including the new `ui_last_helper.txt`, which walks up, asks it home, and looks at the map and notes. I inspected its screenshots.
- The What's next card, where the question appears, used to show only in real games. It now also shows in a scenario while the helper is waiting for an answer, or the scenario couldn't ask it.
- **The first full check caught a stranded start.** The scenario put the player in the Hollow Grove without the Forest Crossing that a real game needs to reach it, and `stranded.rs` checks every scenario starts on open ground. The scenario now has the crossing built.
- The save format stays at 9. The new field defaults to empty, and a test checks the answer survives a save.

## Not verified

- **It appears by the fire at once,** rather than walking home behind you. The words say it follows you.
- Whether anyone goes back to the Last Stone after reading it, to see who's there.
- Whether the choice means anything to a player, when it changes nothing they can count.
