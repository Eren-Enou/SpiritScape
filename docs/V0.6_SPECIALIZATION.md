# v0.6: Spirit specialization and production expansion

This milestone tests one question: *does the player feel rewarded for developing
both their character and their automated settlement?*

It adds a top tier to both skills, real limits on how many can work at a bench,
further workshops, several production orders at once, simple instructions for
each spirit, and infrastructure that makes retraining faster.

## How to try it

The panel's **Special**, **Bottleneck**, **Expansion** and **Retraining** buttons
load these, or use `--scenario`:

```bash
cargo run --release -- --scenario advanced_specialization
```

```bash
cargo run --release -- --scenario production_bottleneck --production
```

```bash
cargo run --release -- --scenario expanded_production --production
```

```bash
cargo run --release -- --scenario supported_retraining
```

Without graphics, repeating exactly:

```bash
cargo run -p spirit_sim --release --example specialization
```

`O` opens the new Spirits tab. `P`, `T` and `J` work as before.

## Advanced tiers

Requirements are the same for the player and for spirits.

| Skill | Level | Unlock | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woodcutting | 10 | **Ironwood** trees | Gives **Hardwood Logs**, a separate resource. 60 XP per log, 6 logs per tree, regrows in 5 minutes |
| Woodworking | 10 | **Structural Beams** | 2 Hardwood Logs make 1 beam. 120 XP, 6 seconds. Workshops cost beams |

Ironwood grows at **Old Growth**, a new site in the north-east. It's reached on
foot over the bridge, about 45 tiles from Main Storage.

Level 10 is 1,154 XP in the development profile and 16,000 XP in normal.

**Soul Separation** needed no new code. The comparison now offers "Ironwood
spirit" and similar options, and the preview lists what's lost. A level 10
spirit keeps the tier for good, and the player loses it until they retrain.

Empty hands at a storage now pick up the input of the best recipe you can make,
so a level 10 Woodworker takes Hardwood Logs rather than Logs.

## Workstation capacity

A Workshop has **2 places** (`slots` in `data/defs.toml`). Anyone with a bench job
holds a place, including while walking to it. This is worked out from task queues,
so there's no extra state to save or get out of step.

When every place is taken, a spirit tries its other permitted workshops, then
another order it can serve, and otherwise waits. It says so: "Woodworking Spirit
waiting: Workshop is occupied."

**Your own access:** the player takes a place like anyone else. If the bench is
full you're told so. **Keep a bench place** in the Training tab makes spirits leave
one free, and if one had already taken it, the last spirit steps away when you
click. With this on, a basic Workshop runs one spirit, so it has a real cost.

## More workshops

**Place Workshop** in the Production tab costs 20 Logs, 20 Planks and 6 Structural
Beams. Press it, then click a free tile. The tile must be buildable, reachable and
not cut anything off. You can have 3. The Construction Spirit builds it like any
other project.

A new Workshop is an independent site with its own stock, places and output
count. Workers go to whichever has a free place, and haulers supply and collect
from all of them.

## Production orders

Each product has its own order: target, input reserve, on/off and priority. Today
that's Planks (the main order) and Structural Beams. More can be added with
`more_outputs` in `data/defs.toml`, though the UI's buttons are written for beams.

| Example | How it's set |
|---|---|
| Maintain 50 Planks | The plank target |
| Maintain 20 Beams | The beam target |
| Reserve 30 Logs | The log reserve, respected by production and training supply |

**Dependency handling** is deliberately small:

- Each order's shortfall becomes requests for inputs at stations, for haulers.
- It also adds a capped amount to the gather goal of each input, for gatherers. Hardwood has no goal of its own, so nobody cuts Ironwood until something needs it.
- A project's unreserved cost adds to the order for that product, so ordering a Workshop makes beams if needed.
- **No recursion:** planning looks one step upstream. `GameDefs` refuses recipes that form a loop when data loads, so circular demand can't exist.
- **No duplication:** workers only move stock through existing tasks, and conservation is checked in tests on every tick.
- **No over-reserving:** a trip reserves only what it will carry and releases it on failure.
- **No oscillation:** targets stop work with a per-tick budget, the same way v0.4 did. A test checks the line settles and stays settled.

**Priorities:** higher priority goes first. Between equals, a spirit takes the
hardest work it's qualified for, because fewer spirits can do it. Between batches,
a worker moves to a more urgent order if it can start one where it stands.

## Spirit instructions

The Spirits tab (`O`) has simple controls for one spirit at a time:

| Control | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Focus** | Anything, Settlement work (no training support), My training (training support only), or Rest |
| **Gather at** | One site, or anywhere |
| **Work at** | One workshop, or any |
| **Top tier first** | Gather the best material in demand before anything else |

Instructions narrow what a spirit does and never widen it. A level 5 gatherer told
to work at Old Growth with Top tier first just waits, because it can't cut
Ironwood. A spirit with no permitted work stays idle and says why.

More advanced control is in the Production tab: order targets, priority, and
which workers are on production.

## Training through infrastructure

v0.5 found that support saved clicks but could cost time. v0.6 adds two things to
change that:

- **Log Rack** (10 Planks, 10 Logs, up to 2): each adds 40 logs of bench space for you, so a good stock can wait there.
- **My training focus:** a hauler serves you only, and a gatherer keeps an extra bench's worth of logs in stock.

Slow, low-attention training is still an option: "Hauling support" alone takes
longer, with almost no clicks.

## Measured results

From `examples/specialization.rs`. The reference player has a one-second reaction
time and never hesitates, so **these are lower bounds, not a person's times.**
Development rates.

**Scenario B: more workers on one Workshop.**

| Workers | Planks per minute | Limit named |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34 | Processing speed |
| 2 | 68 | Processing speed |
| 3 | 68 | Workshop capacity |
| 4 | 68 | Workshop capacity |

**Scenario C: a second Workshop.** With four workers and enough logs, output rose
from **68 to 140 planks a minute**. It opened after 30 seconds, and both sites
produced. In that scenario beams and planks ran together until the beam target was
met.

**Scenario D: retraining from the same start.**

| Woodworking, 3,000 XP | Time | XP/min | Clicks | Storage trips | Idle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No assistance | 4:44 | 633 | 41 | 23 | 4% |
| Hauling support | 6:51 | 437 | 5 | 0 | 49% |
| Dedicated gatherer and hauler | 6:38 | 452 | 6 | 0 | 47% |
| Plus Log Racks and a standing stock | 3:27 | **869** | **1** | 0 | 0% |

| Woodcutting, 2,000 XP | Time | XP/min | Clicks | Storage trips | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No assistance | 5:42 | 350 | 49 | 8 | 20% |
| Grove reserved | 5:32 | 361 | 48 | 9 | 20% |
| Plus Training Cache and a dedicated hauler | 5:14 | 382 | 53 | 14 | 12% |

**What this shows:**

- **Investment now pays off for Woodworking.** Fully built, training is 37% faster than alone with 1 click instead of 41. That stock was built up beforehand, and the time and materials for it aren't in the table.
- **A dedicated gatherer alone barely helps** (437 to 452). The limit is logs arriving, so stored stock is what matters.
- **Woodcutting gains stay modest:** 9% faster. It's mostly chopping, which nothing should do for you.
- **The first-cycle pace is unchanged.** The v0.5 normal milestones repeat exactly.

## Logistics information

The Production tab now shows:

| Card | Contents |
|---|---|
| What limits planks | The current limit and what would help |
| Orders | Stock, goal, rate per minute and a sentence per order |
| Workshops | Places in use, who's there, units made, haulers' walking share |
| Production line | As before |

The sentences are specific, for example:

- "Structural Beams production paused: insufficient Hardwood Logs."
- "Two Woodworking Spirits are idle. Additional workshop capacity would increase output."
- "Structural Beams waiting: hauling capacity is limiting Hardwood Logs delivery."
- "Structural Beams paused: needs a Woodworking Spirit of level 10 on production."

The Spirits tab adds what one spirit is doing and its recent walking and waiting.

## Save compatibility

- The save format is now **version 2**. Version 1 (v0.5) saves load and are upgraded.
- **What upgrading does:** adds the Old Growth trees (skipping any tile that's been built on), opens the new site, adds the beam order with a target of 0, and makes room at Workshops. New fields take safe defaults. Progress, spirits, jobs and projects aren't changed.
- **The original is kept** as `world.save.v1` or `autosave.save.v1` the first time it's upgraded, and the game says so.
- **Tested against real files:** `tests/fixtures/` holds two saves written by the v0.5 code before any v0.6 change. They load, conserve resources, finish their project and continue deterministically.
- Saves from a newer format are still refused rather than misread. `*.save` is marked binary in Git so line endings can't break the fixtures' checksums.

## Technical debt

- **Training logs live only at the first Workshop.** `bench_stock` uses `station()`, so a second Workshop has no player stock.
- **The main order and `extra_orders` are separate fields,** kept so older tests and saves stayed valid. One list would be cleaner, in a later save format.
- **The UI's order buttons say "Beams"**, and there's one row, so a third product needs generalizing.
- **`Task::Craft.output` is `None` for the player,** who makes the best thing their materials allow. There's no way to choose planks when you could make beams.
- **`keep_bench_place` applies to every Workshop**, not one.
- **Stations are chosen in id order**, not by distance.
- **A hauler that's dedicated to training still collects all finished goods,** not only the player's.
- **"Workshop waiting: insufficient logs"** in `WorkshopStatus` is still about the main order only. Per-order sentences are in the Orders card.
- **The player's bag can jam at a full bench** when both are full of planks. It stops crafting but doesn't explain why.

## Unresolved questions

1. **Is level 10 in reach?** At the measured 350-440 XP a minute, 16,000 XP is roughly 40 minutes of chopping. That's an estimate, and it's a long way past the first session.
2. **Main Storage limits solo training.** 16,000 XP is over 1,000 logs against 300 capacity. Reaching level 10 needs you to spend logs or build Storehouses, which may be good or may be a wall.
3. **Is Old Growth too far?** A level 10 spirit is expensive, then spends much of its time walking. A Field Storage may be needed there.
4. **Are 2 places right?** They make Keep a bench place expensive on a single Workshop.
5. **Do beams have enough uses?** Only Workshops need them so far.
6. **Does production stay comfortable?** Tests show it settles, but not whether it's pleasant to live with.
