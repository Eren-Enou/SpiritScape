# v0.47: Kept company

**Player problem.** Since v0.46, spirits notice each other in small moments: "Oakroot waited for Mossbell at the bend in the path." But each moment was a one-off. Nothing gathered them into anything, so two spirits who kept turning up in each other's moments were no closer for it, and neither ending knew.

**Now two spirits who keep sharing moments come to keep each other company, and both endings remember it.** Nobody has played it.

## Design

### How it starts

Every moment that names another spirit counts as one shared between those two. When two spirits have shared two, they keep each other company:

> Oakroot and Fernlight keep each other company now.

That goes in the event log, as a notice, and in a new section of the notes, "Who keeps company".

**Each spirit keeps company with one other, and it lasts.** If anyone could pair with everyone, it would mean nothing. The first pair to get there is the pair.

### What remembers it

- **Their story** (Spirits tab, `O`): "Has kept company with Fernlight since 2:00." It uses the companion's name as it is now, so renaming one shows in the other's story.
- **The Hearth:** "Oakroot and Fernlight stood together by the Hearth." Up to three pairs.
- **The Still Boat:** once one of a pair is aboard, **the boat waits for the other,** and the closing words say "Oakroot and Fernlight crossed together." This is the one place it changes what happens, and it's in the ending, where nothing is at stake but how it reads. If the time runs out, everyone boards anyway.

### What I decided

- **It changes nothing in the economy.** Moments never have, and the old stones' point is that the helpers were never more than useful. A bonus for working near your companion would turn this into a placement puzzle.
- **The player can't make or break a bond.** You can only notice it. That keeps it something that happened, not something you did.
- **Two shared moments.** In the reference game, seven pairs get there, but only two bonds form, because the first pair wins and each spirit has one companion. One threshold lower and almost everyone would pair off in the first hour. Higher, and the late spirits would never get the chance.
- **The rule and every line of text are in `data/defs.toml`, under `[bonds]`.** Setting `moments = 0` turns companions off.

### A scenario to see it

`old_friends` (number 14 in `scenarios.bat`) starts with two spirits already keeping company, so you can read their stories without playing two hours.

**What would count as failure:** a spirit with two companions, a bond that names a spirit who isn't there, the boat stuck waiting, or a placeholder showing in the text.

## Measured

The reference game, all goals:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| All 19 goals | 16:57:21 | 16:57:21 |
| Small moments | 101 | 101 |
| Keeping company | none | Oakroot and Fernlight at 2:00, Mossbell and Thistledown at 11:00 |

The game is unchanged, because companions change nothing it measures. The first bond comes two hours in, which is about when a player has two or three spirits and might start telling them apart.

Moments shared, by pair, in that game: 6, 3, 3, 3, 1, 2, 4, 1, 2.

## Validation

- `cargo test --workspace`: **644 pass**, 635 from before plus 9 new.
- Eight release-only long-run tests pass.
- `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` is clean.
- **All 42 UI scripts pass,** including the new `ui_companions.txt`. I inspected its screenshots.
- Two reference games are identical.
- The save format stays at 9. Every new field defaults to empty, and `companions_survive_a_save` checks a round trip.

## Not verified

- **Whether anyone notices.** One line in the log at 2:00 is easy to miss.
- Whether "keep each other company" is the right weight: warmer than a moment, lighter than a friendship.
- The boat waiting for a companion has only been seen in a test, not on screen.

## Human playtest

Play a real game for two or three hours, or run `scenarios.bat` and choose 14.

- [ ] Did two of your spirits come to keep each other company? Did you notice, and did you care?
- [ ] At the ending, did the line about them land?
