# v0.16: Ambitions

**Player problem.** After the first cycle, the guidance said "What comes next is up to you". Everything added since v0.9 had no path into it: nothing in a normal game pointed at roads, panels, the quarry or the crossing.

**There's now always a next thing to look at.** Twelve long-term goals sit at the top of the Progress tab (`J`), and a settled game's "What's next" card points at the first one not yet met. Nobody has played it, so whether these are goals a person wants is unknown.

## Design

- **Data, not code:** goals are `[[ambitions]]` in `data/defs.toml`. Each lists buildings to finish, a skill level, or an amount to make. A goal naming something unknown is refused when data loads.
- **Watches, never gates:** like the journey, goals reward nothing and block nothing, and they can be met in any order.
- **Nothing is saved:** goals are worked out from the world each time, so the save format doesn't change, and older saves get credit for what they've done.
- **Skill goals use your best level ever,** so giving XP to a spirit doesn't undo one.
- **Storage trouble still comes first:** the storage message replaces the hint while it applies.

### Rejected

- **Rewards for finishing a goal:** the things being built are the reward, and bonuses would need their own balance.
- **A notice when a goal is met:** I'd need to save which ones have been announced. It's a small follow-up if people want it.

**What would count as failure:** a goal changing the world, or a goal a player can't reach. Whether the goals are worth having is for a person to say.

## Measured

Reproduce with `cargo run -p spirit_sim --release --example guided_report`.

| Reference run, ending about 2:30 in | Goals met |
|---|---|
| Hand-written habits | 6 of 12 |
| Message only | 7 of 12 |

The other goals are possible but aren't on that route. The tests cover Quarrying and the causeway separately. No test reaches level 20, so the Master goals are unproven. At the panel run's 450 XP a minute, Woodworking 20 is several hours away.

Goals don't change a run: a test confirms the world's state is identical with and without reading them.
