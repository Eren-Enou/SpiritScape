# v0.41: What they leave

**Player problem.** v0.37 showed that taking all six offers saves about 2 minutes in 17 hours. Offers read like choices, but they hardly registered, and the closing story never mentioned anyone you'd met.

**Now people leave something behind.** Nobody has played it.

## Design

### Lessons

Nine of the visitors teach you something if you take their offer. It's real XP in a real skill: 150 Woodcutting from the pedlar, 1,500 Masonry from the old mason. The offer says you'd learn something, but not how much.

Turning someone down teaches you nothing, and a visitor who leaves unanswered teaches you nothing either.

### The closing story

Most visitors now have a line for the end, and some have a different one if you turned them down:

- "The pilgrim got home."
- "You never did find out if the pilgrim got home."
- "The old mason never did cut that last keystone."
- "The pedlar's daughter married her joiner. There's a bench of yours at the wedding."

Both endings use them. Only the latest three appear, because a closing story isn't a ledger. To make room, each ending now names four spirits rather than six.

### Why I didn't make lessons bigger

Offers now save 8 minutes rather than 2, which is still small. I could have raised that a lot, but the rule for visitors is that an offer should never be simply better than saying no. If lessons were big enough to show up in a 17-hour total, refusing would just be a mistake. So they're sized to matter when they arrive, and the lasting part is what the story says about you.

A lesson matters more early: the pedlar's 150 XP comes when level 3 only takes 380.

**What would count as failure:** an offer that's always right, or a closing story too long to read.

## Not verified

- **Whether anyone remembers the pilgrim 12 hours later.** The line only lands if they do.
- Whether "You'd learn something" makes people say yes too easily.
- How the longest closing story looks. With everything built, it's about 15 lines. It should fit a normal window, but I haven't seen that exact case on screen.

## Human playtest

Play through to an ending having answered a few visitors.

- [ ] Did a closing line take you back to something you'd decided?
- [ ] Did you feel bad about anyone you turned down?
